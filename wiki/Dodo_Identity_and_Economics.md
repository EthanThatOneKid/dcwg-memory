On 2026-10-18, `Sandeep Kumar Kuanar✌🏼` proposed a unified identity for Dodo as an embeddable runtime, "Tauri for Web Apps." This proposal suggests a modular core (the "LEGO" approach) that strips out components like the linter and formatter to serve as an ultimate engine for tools such as Tauri, desktop applications, and local-first tools. `Sandeep Kumar Kuanar✌🏼` identified the "Deploy Void" (how hosting works for an embedded runtime) and a JITless strategy (for platforms like iOS/Switch) as missing links, and raised questions about the "Economic Engine" required to fund maintainers, exploring options like monetizing embeddability for enterprise or building a hosted service. `Sandeep Kumar Kuanar✌🏼` also provided a PDF white paper (`Dodo_and_Ende.pdf`).

Also on 2026-10-18, `daniellacosse` proposed an analogy: Node.js is to the Unreal Engine what Dodo/Deno can be to Unity (`1561457200470302862`). `w` later refined this, suggesting Node.js is to Unity what Dodo/Deno is to Godot (`1561459653421039772`), an analogy `daniellacosse` agreed was probably more accurate (`1561459966181638329`).

`daniellacosse` addressed the JITless concern with two potential approaches: developing a lightweight system and offloading tasks to WebGPU compute shaders, noting a proposal for this is approximately 70% drafted. Regarding monetization, `daniellacosse` considered hosting as "moot" due to market saturation and capital requirements. Alternative models suggested included private console NDA support (deemed risky, potentially turning the project into a game development company), which `daniellacosse` stated would require a legal entity to sign NDAs and that any proper extension would need to be opaque to developers and reside in a private repository, similar to how Unity operates (`1561457587092848754`; `1561459321848725555`). `CaptainLach` clarified that the MIT license permits the creation of proprietary versions of the code, and noted that Deno already uses binary blobs, explaining how NDAs can coexist with an MIT-licensed project (`1561459994652577893`). Other monetization options included accepting donations or offering bounties (compatible with a volunteer-driven model), or providing consulting services (which could distract from core development). `daniellacosse` stressed that significant investments can shift project ownership away from the original team, citing Deno's past experience, and emphasized that building trust is a prerequisite for fundraising. `daniellacosse` also proposed amending the core architecture path to "cli <-> core <-> TS runtime <-> engine" and mentioned the possibility of "bart suddenly deciding 'okay actually, let's make Deno a foundation now'" as an external variable.

`CaptainLach` questioned the practical utility of a Deno foundation if there were no developers actively working on Deno's side, pointing out that Deno's development historically relied heavily on employees. `CaptainLach` did not foresee a foundation being established by anyone other than Cloudflare. `w` expressed apprehension about the project being rolled into a foundation, stating that Deno hacking was a form of relaxation, and that donations inevitably introduce obligations. `w` also noted a "chicken and egg problem" in cultivating a userbase if the project's strategy wasn't compatible with the idea of a foundation. `daniellacosse` agreed with `w`'s concerns but acknowledged that not everyone has the luxury of working without such obligations. `CaptainLach` suggested making Deno largely self-maintaining by delegating more work to external libraries, such as replacing Laufey backends with Tauri or rewriting cryptography components with higher-level Rust libraries. `CaptainLach` identified Node.js compatibility as the most expensive part of the codebase due to its "hazmat APIs" and associated vulnerability risks. `daniellacosse` pondered the possibility of raising funds specifically to maintain Node.js compatibility. `w` suggested a "wait and see" approach for Node.js compatibility maintenance, noting that Node.js does not evolve at a rapid pace, but `CaptainLach` countered that serious maintenance is crucial for hazmat APIs due to security vulnerabilities.

An off-topic discussion occurred between `CaptainLach`, `daniellacosse`, and `w` regarding the quality of Google API autogenerated code, particularly from `protoc` and `grpc`. Separately, `w`, `daniellacosse`, `andy`, and `CaptainLach` discussed Discord channel permissions for `#github` and the potential creation of an `#offtop` channel.
