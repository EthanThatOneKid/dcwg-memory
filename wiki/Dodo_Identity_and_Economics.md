On 2026-10-18, `Sandeep Kumar Kuanar✌🏼` articulated Dodo's identity as "the embeddable, predictable runtime for the next generation of cross-platform tools," distinguishing it from Node or Deno replacements and positioning it as a foundation for builders. He broke this vision down into three layers: 1) The Core (highly embeddable, modular, for use in Tauri, desktop, or CLI apps); 2) The DX (predictable, deterministic, standards-based, and accommodating JITless environments like iPad or Switch); and 3) The Ecosystem (a lightweight way to distribute apps, local-first or edge-first by default, without monolithic cloud hosts). `Sandeep Kumar Kuanar✌🏼` stated Dodo's differentiator is being the only runtime treating JavaScript as a first-class citizen for embedding (1561488156547621058). He then announced he would start a new thread in the #propose channel to standardize this (1561488293198041211). This expanded on his earlier proposal for a unified identity as an embeddable runtime, "Tauri for Web Apps," suggesting a modular core (the "LEGO" approach) that strips out components like the linter and formatter to serve as an ultimate engine for tools such as Tauri, desktop applications, and local-first tools. `Sandeep Kumar Kuanar✌🏼` had previously identified the "Deploy Void" (how hosting works for an embedded runtime) and a JITless strategy (for platforms like iOS/Switch) as missing links, and raised questions about the "Economic Engine" required to fund maintainers, exploring options like monetizing embeddability for enterprise or building a hosted service. `Sandeep Kumar Kuanar✌🏼` also provided a PDF white paper (`Dodo_and_Ende.pdf`).

Also on 2026-10-18, `daniellacosse` proposed an analogy: Node.js is to the Unreal Engine what Dodo/Deno can be to Unity (`1561457200470302862`). `w` later refined this, suggesting Node.js is to Unity what Dodo/Deno is to Godot (`1561459653421039772`), an analogy `daniellacosse` agreed was probably more accurate (`1561459966181638329`).

`daniellacosse` addressed the JITless concern with two potential approaches: developing a lightweight system and offloading tasks to WebGPU compute shaders, noting a proposal for this is approximately 70% drafted. Regarding monetization, `daniellacosse` considered hosting as "moot" due to market saturation and capital requirements. Alternative models suggested included private console NDA support (deemed risky, potentially turning the project into a game development company), which `daniellacosse` stated would require a legal entity to sign NDAs and that any proper extension would need to be opaque to developers and reside in a private repository, similar to how Unity operates (`1561457587092848754`; `1561459321848725555`). `CaptainLach` clarified that the MIT license permits the creation of proprietary versions of the code, and noted that Deno already uses binary blobs, explaining how NDAs can coexist with an MIT-licensed project (`1561459994652577893`). Other monetization options included accepting donations or offering bounties (compatible with a volunteer-driven model), or providing consulting services (which could distract from core development). `daniellacosse` stressed that significant investments can shift project ownership away from the original team, citing Deno's past experience, and emphasized that building trust is a prerequisite for fundraising. `daniellacosse` also proposed amending the core architecture path to "cli <-> core <-> TS runtime <-> engine" and mentioned the possibility of "bart suddenly deciding 'okay actually, let's make Deno a foundation now'" as an external variable.

`CaptainLach` questioned the practical utility of a Deno foundation if there were no developers actively working on Deno's side, pointing out that Deno's development historically relied heavily on employees. `CaptainLach` did not foresee a foundation being established by anyone other than Cloudflare. `w` expressed apprehension about the project being rolled into a foundation, stating that Deno hacking was a form of relaxation, and that donations inevitably introduce obligations. `w` also noted a "chicken and egg problem" in cultivating a userbase if the project's strategy wasn't compatible with the idea of a foundation. `daniellacosse` agreed with `w`'s concerns but acknowledged that not everyone has the luxury of working without such obligations. `CaptainLach` suggested making Deno largely self-maintaining by delegating more work to external libraries, such as replacing Laufey backends with Tauri or rewriting cryptography components with higher-level Rust libraries. `CaptainLach` identified Node.js compatibility as the most expensive part of the codebase due to its "hazmat APIs" and associated vulnerability risks. `daniellacosse` pondered the possibility of raising funds specifically to maintain Node.js compatibility. `w` suggested a "wait and see" approach for Node.js compatibility maintenance, noting that Node.js does not evolve at a rapid pace, but `CaptainLach` countered that serious maintenance is crucial for hazmat APIs due to security vulnerabilities.

## Project Direction and Architecture

On 2026-10-19, `Sandeep Kumar Kuanar✌🏼`, replying to `CaptainLach` regarding delegating work to external libraries, raised three problems: deciding which libraries to use, ensuring dependencies are updated and security-checked (CVEs), and mapping new features to new projects. `Sandeep Kumar Kuanar✌🏼` suggested solving this by building and maintaining packages that Dodo/Ende would use, to delegate risk to maintainers, noting this approach is risky but modular, citing Deno's use of Fresh. `Sandeep Kumar Kuanar✌🏼` also agreed that ad-hoc patches would be a time sink, as they were for the Deno team. `Sandeep Kumar Kuanar✌🏼` liked the idea of deferring 'deploy' to later.

`Sandeep Kumar Kuanar✌🏼` agreed with a participant's (handle 400711308141658112) thinking in terms of 'layers of opinionated default, unopinionated escape hatch,' while highlighting the challenge of defining boundaries between core, extensions, and essential components. On 2026-10-19, `Sandeep Kumar Kuanar✌🏼` agreed with a participant (handle 184349064161132563) regarding the fear of 'jumping the gun' to commit to a foundation too early, given Dodo's early experimental stage. `Sandeep Kumar Kuanar✌🏼` proposed a tentative release schedule for Dodo/Ende to be 'shown to the world,' stating this would provide a clear roadmap with deadlines (aiding scope and resource planning) and clear V1 definitions for both projects. `Sandeep Kumar Kuanar✌🏼` observed a participant (handle 370871659839619072) was anxious about Dodo becoming a 'bloated framework' rather than an 'embeddable engine,' and invited further discussion.

On 2026-10-19, `netop://ウィビ` asked if a high-level overview of Deno's features and what to keep had been done, stating that drawing things out makes planning easier and many conversations seem like 'unnecessary waffling.' `netop://ウィビ` recalled that Dodo was intended to be a 'drop-in, maintained replacement for Deno,' which `Sandeep Kumar Kuanar✌🏼` confirmed on 2026-10-19. `netop://ウィビ` recommended figuring out Dodo's identity first, getting CI running after changing 'Deno' to 'Dodo' across the codebase, and then moving on, describing this as 'common sense product management,' which `Sandeep Kumar Kuanar✌🏼` noted for documentation.

`Sandeep Kumar Kuanar✌🏼` clarified on 2026-10-19 that 'maintaining packages' referred to a 'Trait/Adapter pattern,' not a JSR-like registry, illustrating with an iOS/WebKit example where the core defines an interface and community members maintain adapters (e.g., `WebKitAdapter`) to distribute maintenance and prevent burnout. `w` commented on 2026-10-19 that different owners for codebase parts is a good thought experiment, but noted the WebKit API example might be less apt due to `v8x`. `Sandeep Kumar Kuanar✌🏼` inquired 'what is v8x? and why there is not v9?'. `w` also stressed on 2026-10-19 that open-source projects have participants who 'dip in and dip out,' making it undesirable to have a bus factor of 1 on important components.

`CaptainLach` clarified on 2026-10-19 his previous point about higher-level libraries: for laufey, he suggested replacing most backends with `wry` (which Tauri uses) or contributing CEF support to `wry`, stating embedded CEF is the only part not handled by `wry`. `CaptainLach` claimed this would 'greatly reduce maintenance burden' for Deno desktop/mobile. `Sandeep Kumar Kuanar✌🏼` agreed with this approach on 2026-10-19 due to being 'short on people.' `w` simplified the strategy on 2026-10-19 as finding 'another well maintained package that abstracts across various this-or-that-specific apis, use these, make simple,' rather than specializing. `Sandeep Kumar Kuanar✌🏼` identified on 2026-10-19 'Dependency Risk' as a trade-off, inheriting bugs, release cycles, and limitations from third-party crates. `CaptainLach` countered on 2026-10-19 that a third-party crate would be 'much better maintained than ours' without a large team of paid people, especially if it's used in projects like Tauri. `w` added on 2026-10-19 'and their sweet sweet cve patches' as a benefit of third-party dependencies. `Sandeep Kumar Kuanar✌🏼` conceded on 2026-10-19 'Fair point, exactly my worry. Like there is just one way to go.'

On 2026-10-20, `daniellacosse` believed the discussions implied Dodo was still a drop-in replacement, requiring 'everything' to be kept. `daniellacosse` referred `netop://ウィビ` to an existing roadmap (Discord channel `#1560371677127381232`), stating an effort to 'keep it simple so as to not overplan,' but also mentioning a desire to 'diagram out the codebase' without having had the time. `netop://ウィビ` stated on 2026-10-20 an intention to review the roadmap.

## The foundation, and the AI-use tangent

The email itself is not in this capture.

He set out the two paths he saw if a foundation forms: join it and keep developing as the group is, or not join while Deno survives anyway, leaving Dodo and Ende as separate entities with an unclear future. `daniellacosse` said the group needs to wait and see what "Bart" says and that those are the paths forward. This matches the holding pattern recorded in [Sandboxed extension boundary](Sandboxed_Extension_Boundary.md). The foundation is described by `daniellacosse` only as possible; nothing here confirms it.
